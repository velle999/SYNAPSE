The desktop grows up. 0.2.6 gave the settings panels a mouse and a control
panel worth opening; this one is about everything those panels point at — a
theme that reaches past synui into the applications, three new window layouts,
an AI model you can pick and download instead of one you inherit, and a
security toolchain that was never on the ISO before.

105 commits, 66 synui releases, three new packages.

## The theme finally leaves the compositor

Picking a theme used to repaint synui and stop there. The bar took the accent
and the compositor's own panels did not; Dolphin kept whatever KDE had decided;
kitty and rofi were never in the conversation at all. So a "theme" was really
a bar colour with a desktop full of applications disagreeing with it.

`synctl dispatch theme <accent> <surface> <ink>` now repaints synui's own
panels and window borders, and the same palette is written out for the
applications that can read one: Dolphin and the rest of KF6 (as a real `.colors`
scheme, with a `KGlobalSettings` change notification so it lands **without
restarting the app**), kitty, and rofi. Qt is given a platform theme, which is
what "Dolphin drew its filenames in black on a black background" turned out to
be — with no platform theme Qt falls back to a palette that assumes a light
desktop.

Two of these were worse than cosmetic. A theme switch was **OOM-killing every
open KDE app** — and a switch left Dolphin's *icons* on the old theme even when
its colours changed.

**Pale themes are legible now.** The ink ladder and the status colours were
picked once, against a black panel, and then used on every theme — so a light
scheme rendered its own secondary text somewhere between faint and invisible.
The lower half of the ladder is floored on pale panels and accents are
contrast-checked against the surface they land on rather than assumed.

**A config reload no longer resets the theme.** `theme.state` was the one file
`synui_config_load()` did not read, so `Super+Shift+W` — a full reload — quietly
put the default palette back.

## The Antiquity shell

There are **two bars** now. `bar_shell` in synuirc picks; Antiquity is the
second, with a palette of its own that brings its own wallpaper. Its settings,
Super-tap, control panel and bar all work as the default shell's do — a second
shell that behaved differently would be its own bug.

## Three more ways to arrange windows

Super+Tab walked four layouts. It walks seven.

**niri** — an endless horizontal strip of columns per desktop-and-monitor. A new
window opens in its own column *beside* the one you are in and the strip gets
longer; nothing already on screen is made narrower, which is the whole
difference from the master-stack tiler. Super+, and Super+. consume and expel a
column, and the strip scrolls to follow the focus.

**spiral** — Fibonacci tiling, each window taking half of what is left, winding
inward. It trades area for shape, and the trade is the opposite of the obvious
guess: its smallest window is *smaller* than master-stack's, because halving
compounds. What it buys is that nothing ever becomes a letterbox — master-stack's
slot reaches 6.2:1 at nine windows, the spiral stays under 2:1 forever.

**cascade** (Super+Shift+Y) — overlapping windows offset down-and-right so every
titlebar stays reachable, and the pile splits into several once one would run off
the edge. A few hands of cards dealt side by side, rather than one fifty-two-card
slide off the desk. The only layout whose windows overlap on purpose, and the
answer to eight windows being either eight letterbox strips or eight postage
stamps.

**Floating got a tiler too** — `Super+Shift+T` retiles a floating desktop into a
grid instead of leaving `layout_apply` a no-op, and it now switches from any
layout. And the layout you chose is **still there next login**.

## Mission control, and a shortcut palette

**Alt+Tab is mission control.** Every window scaled down and laid out so none of
them overlap, with the virtual desktops as pills along the bottom. It is not a
bigger Alt+Tab and the difference is what each is for: the switcher answers "the
window I was just in" (MRU, a fixed grid, up while a key is held), this answers
"where did I put it", which is a spatial question — so the order is stacking
order and the tiles take the whole output. `alt_tab_style` picks. Super+X is
free again.

**Super+/ opens every keybinding as a list you can type into.** Type to narrow,
Enter runs it. It is not a second list — both it and the control panel's
Shortcuts page walk the live bind table, because a hand-maintained shortcut list
drifts and this project has shipped that bug once already. **Then F2 rebinds the
shortcut you are looking at.**

## An AI model you choose

synapd ran whatever model it was handed, with sampling settings and a chat
format guessed once and applied to everything.

You can now **browse, download and delete models from the control panel**, with
each one described before you commit to a multi-gigabyte download — what it is
for, not just its name. synapd reads the model's **own turn format** out of the
GGUF rather than assuming one, picks sampling settings that suit it, and can
**change model without restarting the daemon**. The pick persists (an `/etc`
tmpfiles entry was shadowing synapd's, so it never did), and a switch that
*fails* now ends rather than looking like no switch happened.

Underneath, **llama.cpp moves b8272 → b10241**, which is what lets newer
architectures load at all. synapd is rebuilt against those headers — on the old
ones it crash-looped on SIGSEGV. synapd also serves retrieval embeddings
(`SYN_MSG_EMBED`) now, which is what chibi's Thoth uses instead of a second
model.

## SYNAPSE Arsenal

New package, and the first security tooling on the ISO. `[blackarch]` is enabled
by default on Standard and Full installs — **the repository is the feature; the
~5000 tools in it are not installed**. Enabling it costs a database sync and a
keyring, which is why it defaults on where Steam does not.

The bootstrap runs upstream's `strap.sh`, which owns the trust handling, but
only after we check it still pins the master-key fingerprint we expect. A pinned
hash of the *script* would be the obvious check and is the wrong one: upstream
edits it routinely, so the pin goes stale and breaks installs that are fine,
while a substituted script that swaps in another key is the actual risk.
`blackarch-keyring` is then installed as a **package**, so key rotations arrive
as an upgrade rather than never.

Arsenal itself is a category browser, GUI and TUI over one backend so the two
cannot disagree about what is installed. `sudo syn arsenal --enable-repo` adds
the repo later if you declined it.

Already fixed once since: **a single dead BlackArch mirror stopped every system
upgrade on the machine**, because pacman treats one unreachable mirror in one
repo as a failure of `-Syu` entirely.

## Installs you can repeat

`syn-install --config` answers the installer's questions from a file, in either
Nix syntax or plain `key = value`. Anything the profile leaves out is still
asked, which is what makes a partial profile useful — pin the disk layout and
the package set, decide the hostname at the machine.

Keys are **semantic, never menu positions**: `filesystem = "btrfs"`, not `2`,
because a number means whatever that row happens to be on the day you install
and menus grow entries. A key that answers nothing is **reported by name** at
the end — `bootlaoder=limine` is not a syntax error, it is silence, and silence
is how preseeding fails.

There is also an **optional Nix + Home Manager layer** (off by default, on in
Full). pacman keeps owning the system; Nix owns a declarative user environment
beside it. The bridge is `facts.nix`, which **probes** the installed machine
rather than taking the installer's answers as input — so add Steam with pacman
next month, re-run it, and the facts follow.

This is not NixOS-style declarative system management, and the README says so
where someone would go looking for it. SynapseOS stays Arch.

**A Full install now brings Steam and the game stack.**

## Things on the desktop

Post-it notes — as many as you want, and one you can paste into. A slice of
pizza that orders you pizza. Nine coats for the desktop cat. Desktop widgets you
can **drag**, by a corner grip, with a chamfered HUD to drag them by.

And **files can be dragged onto the desktop**: let go over the wallpaper and the
drag's files are copied into `~/Desktop`, pinned on the cell you dropped them on.
The desktop is not a `wl_surface`, so there was nothing for a drop to land on —
the compositor is now the drop target itself.

That is the second half of a drag-and-drop fix. The first: **a file dragged out
of Dolphin could not be dropped anywhere at all.** The XWM had been running the
whole session with no seat, which killed the X11 clipboard bridge and
Wayland→X11 drops, both silently.

Also new: an emoji picker, a UI font picker, an image cropper (with a keybind, a
recent-images list, and corners you can actually grab), a graphic equalizer whose
filter chain — it turns out — **could never start**, an active recording shown in
the bar with a stop button, and **fingerprint unlock**, with the enrollment
instructions somewhere you can see them.

Screen recording captures **desktop audio**, not the microphone.

## Branding

The SYN wordmark is replaced by the dendrite mark everywhere it printed,
including the console. About OS is `fetch` (areofyl's, packaged here and taught
what desktop it is on). And the Wallpaper Engine wallpapers on the ISO are
**ours** now — a dendrite piece and a Tux drawn to Larry Ewing's proportions —
rather than requiring Steam to have anything at all.

## Fixes worth naming

- **Suspend no longer dies on the GPU.** An open CUDA context blocked the
  suspend; synapd releases it, and the NVIDIA sleep units are shipped rather
  than remembered.
- **chibi 0.1.0-12** closes 0.2.6's known issue: Thoth had indexed its own
  README (three retrievable passages, one carrying a personal name), and dream
  sync's "no token configured, or the token matches" meant an unset token
  **accepted every request** on a listener bound to all interfaces. Both fixed;
  its launcher now names `/usr/bin/chibi` outright instead of letting `PATH`
  decide between the package and a development checkout.
- The Wallpaper Engine wallpaper was **swallowing every desktop click**.
- Moving the bar turned it solid accent; the control panel reset your font;
  a menu moved after it opened; an X11 window forgot where it was dropped; the
  big clock's shadow was detached from its digits; notifications were cropped.
